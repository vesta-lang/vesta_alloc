The other half of the answer, not a second opinion: whoever
defers the zeroing wins below and loses above.  A row where a
1 MiB request is barely read says more about the caller asking
for the wrong size than about the allocator.

  Reserved is address space and costs nothing until it is
  committed; committed is what the system has to back.  We
  reserve once and keep what we commit, which is why our
  per-row figures fall to zero after the first case: the memory
  is already ours.  That is the price of the speed above.

A ratio above 1.00 means this allocator is that many times
faster than the system's.  A row marked SYSTEM is a real
result, not noise to be explained away -- and one marked
"too close" is the honest answer for a difference this
machine cannot resolve today, not a missing one.
